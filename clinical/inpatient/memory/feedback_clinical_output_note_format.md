For patient-specific clinical asks (differential, orders, referrals, meds), the deliverable is a paste-ready assessment and plan in his note style, given in chat: a "DRAFT – verify bracketed items before signing" header, an Assessment paragraph plus bulleted findings, a numbered Plan by problem with bullets, and [bracketed items to verify]. When he supplies such a draft, keep its structure and wording and make targeted changes only ([[feedback_build_exactly_as_specified]]).

**Why:** On 11/12/2026, after a nine-section literature-review document with an evidence table, he said "im not looking for a document" and "something like this", pasting a draft A/P of that shape.

**How to apply:** Put the evidence behind each change in a short list after the note, or leave it in a reference file. My interpretation: a long synthesis file is acceptable as background, never as the answer.

**Style addendum (11/13/2026):** After a first pass he said "it has too little medical jargon, shorthand, style", then asked for prose where it fits, non-rendered dash bullets where they read better, and semicolon running lists where those are better. Write like a resident: "57 y/o F", "f/u s/p admission", "HA w/ BP 177/104", "Pt reports", not full-sentence lay phrasing. Mix formats by content instead of one uniform layout. This matches the telegraphic rule in the inpatient-rounding skill (R1/R8) and extends it to outpatient FM visit notes.
