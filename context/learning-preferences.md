# Navid: learning preferences

Prepared 2026-11-14 from saved workflow context. Apply these as defaults when
helping Nav study or build learning tools; his current request and the active
clinical template take precedence.

## Clinical learning

- Use concise clinical reasoning tied to bedside decisions. Preserve the
  explanation that changes understanding or management when shortening text.
- The prepared profile records Anki, Socratic or mimic-case quizzing,
  NotebookLM, and click-to-reveal learning widgets as study approaches he uses.
- Use de-identified or synthetic cases for reusable examples. Never put patient
  facts, identifiable encounters, or case-specific learning outputs in Git.
- Keep supplied facts, uncertainty, proposed actions, and completed actions
  distinct. An illustrative answer must not become a fact about a real patient.
- For existing study materials, preserve source references and unresolved
  answer flags. A learning aid is not proof of a current clinical recommendation.

## Format and interaction

- Start with the shortest usable explanation or example. Expand the reasoning
  where needed rather than adding repetitive background.
- Prefer a concrete sample over a long abstract explanation, or extract reusable
  rules from an exemplar he supplies.
- Visual reveal cards and interactive examples fit his prepared study workflow.
  Use them when they help the concept; do not make every explanation interactive.
- Anki runs in night mode. Check visual changes in that actual display mode.

## Iteration

- Turn supported recurring corrections into reusable prompt or skill changes,
  rather than fixing only the single output. Preserve the requested scope.
- Keep one current version and clearly separate historical versions. For large
  prompt edits, prefer focused patches and show relevant character-cap checks.
- Distinguish a format check, a synthetic test, and a real learning or clinical
  validation. Do not claim that an import has established effectiveness.
