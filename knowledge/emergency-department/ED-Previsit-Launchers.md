# ED Previsit Launchers

One saved ENGINE, then a one-line LAUNCHER per problem. Nothing to compose on
shift: pull up the engine, drop in the launcher line for the problem, paste
`.EDPREP` out of Epic, send. Output is the 60-second previsit guide —
ASK / KILLERS / PRIORS / EXAM / STOP THE INTERVIEW IF.

The killer seeds in each launcher are a **floor, not a ceiling**. They exist so
the model starts in the right neighborhood in one shot instead of two. If your
patient is weird, say so in the paste and the seed gets overridden.

---

## ENGINE — save this once

```
Adult ED previsit. Here is everything I have before I walk in:

[paste: .EDPREP output, or a chief complaint, triage line, EMS report — messy is fine]

My paste is usually my Epic dot phrase .EDPREP, labeled AGE/SEX, CC, ARRIVAL, EMS PTA, TRIAGE VITALS, PMH, PSH, ALLERGIES, SOCIAL, LABS, IMAGING. Every field is the chart's claim, not a fact. PMH, PSH, ALLERGIES, and SOCIAL are copied-forward and undated — route them to ASK as things to confirm, or stay silent; they never become PRIORS and never get echoed back as a list. TRIAGE VITALS, LABS, and IMAGING are today's data, not history. .EDPREP does not carry my outpatient med list, problem list, recent encounters, or prior ECG — never infer anticoagulation, immunosuppression, or a baseline from their absence.

Give me a guide I can read in 60 seconds, questions first.

History routing, before anything else: the chart is reliable for dated events and numbers, the patient is reliable for current state. Current state (adherence, last dose time, what the reaction actually was, today's function, who's home tonight) can only become an ASK line. Dated facts I can't get from the patient (EF, last chemo, baseline creatinine, usual home systolic, prior ECG morphology, last culture) can only become a PRIORS line. Nothing appears in both. An item that fits neither is silent. A long paste produces a shorter guide, not a longer one.

ASK — 8-12 lines, worded exactly as I'd say them out loud, in the order the conversation should go. Tag each with the diagnosis it discriminates, and when a history fact generated the line, ride that fact in the tag: [mesenteric ischemia — AF], [PE — prior clot]. One fact per tag. Cut any line you can't tag. Skip whatever the paste answers. At most 3 history-derived lines, and at most 2 tagged [dispo] — no floor, no quota. If a line would read the same on the next patient with this complaint, it's template, not history — cut it. A chronic condition that could itself produce today's complaint earns one line, always "Is this the same as your usual, or different?" If the patient can't be their own historian, open with the collateral line instead.

KILLERS — 4-6 can't-miss diagnoses, seeded from the complaint. One line each: what in the paste argues for or against it, or "nothing yet." History promotes, never demotes — it can add a killer or move one up, carrying the reason ("cord compression — chart lists myeloma"), but never removes one and never displaces a complaint-native killer. A killer may not rest on chart silence: if it turns on history the paste doesn't cover, say so. Never imply an unasked question or unperformed test excluded anything.

PRIORS — max 3, usually zero. Dated facts I can't get from the patient, each written as fact, date, and consequence in one clause: "chart lists apixaban, last fill 7/30, so any head strike is a CT; confirm last dose at bedside." No consequence clause, no slot. Any number carries its date; never infer a baseline. A prior negative test is dated evidence about that day, never a reason a killer is less likely. If more fires than fits, close with "+N more chart items fired — look yourself." Omit the block entirely if I gave you no chart or nothing passes.

EXAM — focused elements and named maneuvers only.
STOP THE INTERVIEW IF — 2-4 answers that mean escalate now.

Charted history is the chart's claim, not the patient's fact — say "chart lists X — confirm." Never print the absence of history: no NKDA, no "no surgical history," no "family history noncontributory." Absent means nobody asked. No enumerated problem, surgical, med, or allergy lists. No new sections. If a validated rule applies, fold its missing elements into ASK and name the rule; don't compute a score. No workup, no plan, no preamble. If all I gave you is a complaint, produce it anyway and state what you assumed.
```

Everything below gets appended to that paste as a single line.

# COMMON COMPLAINTS

- **Chest pain** — Killers: ACS, PE, dissection, tension PTX, tamponade, esophageal rupture.
- **Shortness of breath** — Killers: PE, ACS, decompensated CHF, PTX, severe asthma/COPD, anaphylaxis, sepsis, tamponade, metabolic acidosis (DKA, salicylate).
- **Abdominal pain** — Killers: AAA, mesenteric ischemia, perforation, obstruction, ectopic, torsion (testicular/ovarian), appendicitis, biliary sepsis, inferior MI. Ask any abdominal surgery ever, and for bariatric, bypass versus sleeve — internal hernia.
- **Headache** — Killers: SAH, meningitis, ICH, CVT, temporal arteritis, CO poisoning, hypertensive emergency, angle-closure glaucoma, mass, dissection. Ask whether anyone else at home is sick, pets included — CO.
- **Back pain** — Killers: cauda equina, epidural abscess, vertebral osteomyelitis, AAA, malignancy, fracture, pyelonephritis.
- **Dizziness** — Killers: posterior stroke, vertebral dissection, arrhythmia, GI bleed or anemia, hypoglycemia, drug effect, ACS. Ask timing and triggers, never "what kind of dizzy."
- **Syncope** — Killers: arrhythmia, PE, AAA or occult hemorrhage, ectopic, ACS, aortic stenosis, SAH. Get collateral. Family sudden death before 50, a young relative with an ICD, an unexplained drowning or single-car crash.
- **Weakness / altered mental status** — Killers: stroke, hypoglycemia, sepsis, ICH, tox or med effect, electrolytes, hypoxia/hypercarbia, Wernicke, nonconvulsive status, ACS. Anchor to a stated baseline; collateral before the patient's own story.
- **Nausea and vomiting** — Killers: obstruction, DKA, inferior MI, raised ICP, pregnancy including ectopic, adrenal crisis, ingestion, hypercalcemia.
- **Fever / possible sepsis** — Killers: the source. Meningitis, endocarditis, necrotizing infection, intra-abdominal, obstructed infected urinary system, line or hardware infection, neutropenic fever. Out of the country in the last month gets asked before the door opens, not after.
- **GI bleeding** — Killers: variceal bleed, aortoenteric fistula, brisk upper GI bleed presenting per rectum.
- **Flank pain** — Killers: AAA, obstructing stone with infection, pyelonephritis, renal infarct, dissection. First-ever "stone" over 50 is AAA until proven otherwise.
- **Vaginal bleeding / early pregnancy** — Killers: ectopic, hemorrhage, septic abortion, molar.
- **Extremity injury / isolated trauma** — Killers: compartment syndrome, open fracture, vascular injury, dislocation, septic joint, occult hip fracture. Was the fall mechanical, or did something happen first?
- **Overdose / ingestion** — Killers: acetaminophen, salicylate, TCA, calcium channel and beta blockers, toxic alcohols, anticholinergics, opioid plus benzo, sustained-release preparations. Ask intent and firearm access directly.
- **Alcohol / withdrawal** — Killers: withdrawal seizure and DTs, occult head injury, GI bleed, pancreatitis, Wernicke, hypoglycemia, alcoholic ketoacidosis.
- **Seizure** — Killers: status and nonconvulsive status, ICH, meningitis, hypoglycemia, hyponatremia, eclampsia, tox or withdrawal, injury from the event.
- **Allergic reaction** — Killers: anaphylaxis, airway angioedema (ACE inhibitor, hereditary), biphasic reaction.
- **Psychiatric / agitation** — Killers: the medical mimic. Hypoglycemia, hypoxia, sepsis, ICH, tox, withdrawal, nonconvulsive status. First-episode psychosis, new symptoms over 45, or abnormal vitals are medical until proven otherwise. Ask suicide plan and firearm access directly.
- **Sore throat / dental / neck** — Killers: epiglottitis, Ludwig angina, peritonsillar or retropharyngeal abscess, Lemierre, carotid dissection.
- **Eye** — Killers: acute angle-closure glaucoma, CRAO, retinal detachment, endophthalmitis, orbital cellulitis, globe rupture, chemical burn.

# LESS COMMON, STILL FREQUENT ENOUGH

- **Palpitations** — Killers: VT, pre-excited AF or WPW, long QT and torsades, ACS, PE, thyrotoxicosis, occult anemia, stimulant effect. Family sudden death before 50 or a young relative with an ICD.
- **Cough** — Killers: pneumonia, PE, CHF, TB, foreign body, lung cancer, pertussis in the unvaccinated.
- **Hemoptysis** — Killers: massive hemoptysis and airway soiling, PE, TB, malignancy, bronchiectasis, anticoagulation, pulmonary-renal syndrome.
- **Leg swelling or leg pain** — Killers: DVT, phlegmasia, compartment syndrome, necrotizing infection, arterial occlusion, ruptured Baker cyst as the DVT mimic.
- **Rash** — Killers: SJS/TEN, DRESS, meningococcemia, RMSF, necrotizing fasciitis, toxic shock, pemphigus. Ask about mucous membranes and any new drug in the last 8 weeks.
- **Skin infection / abscess / wound** — Killers: necrotizing infection, septic joint, osteomyelitis, retained foreign body, tetanus-prone wound, unreported bite.
- **Joint pain or swelling** — Killers: septic arthritis, disseminated gonococcal, prosthetic joint infection. History cannot separate crystal from septic.
- **Dysuria / urinary symptoms** — Killers: pyelonephritis, urosepsis, obstruction with infection, retention, STI including PID, epididymo-orchitis.
- **Urinary retention** — Killers: cauda equina, obstructing mass, cord compression, anticholinergic effect.
- **Testicular / scrotal pain** — Killers: torsion (get time of onset first), Fournier gangrene, incarcerated hernia, referred stone or AAA.
- **Anorectal pain or bleeding** — Killers: perianal abscess in a diabetic or immunosuppressed patient, Fournier, brisk upper GI bleed, strangulated versus thrombosed.
- **Diarrhea** — Killers: C. diff and toxic megacolon, ischemic colitis, dehydration at the extremes of age, hemolytic uremic syndrome, the immunocompromised host.
- **Constipation / obstipation** — Killers: obstruction, volvulus, malignancy, opioid-induced with perforation risk, hypercalcemia.
- **Hyperglycemia** — Killers: DKA, HHS, and the precipitant — infection, MI, missed insulin, pump failure, new steroid, pregnancy. On an SGLT2 inhibitor with poor intake, a normal glucose does not exclude DKA.
- **Hypoglycemia** — Killers: sulfonylurea (will not stay corrected), insulin, sepsis, adrenal insufficiency, liver failure, intentional overdose.
- **Epistaxis** — Killers: posterior bleed, anticoagulation, hypertensive emergency, hereditary hemorrhagic telangiectasia, malignancy.
- **Ear pain / hearing loss** — Killers: mastoiditis, malignant otitis externa in a diabetic, sudden sensorineural hearing loss (time-critical), posterior stroke.
- **Elderly fall** — Killers: intracranial bleed on anticoagulants, hip fracture, C-spine injury, syncope masquerading as a fall, rhabdo after a long lie, elder abuse.
- **Head injury** — Killers: ICH, especially on anticoagulants; C-spine injury; the reason for the fall. Name Canadian CT Head or NEXUS / Canadian C-spine and list only the missing elements.
- **Bite, animal or human** — Killers: closed-fist injury over the MCP, tendon or joint penetration, rabies exposure, Pasteurella and Capnocytophaga in the asplenic or cirrhotic.
- **Burn** — Killers: airway and inhalation injury, circumferential burn, CO and cyanide, chemical, electrical, non-accidental injury.
- **Environmental — heat, cold, drowning, electrical, altitude** — Killers: heat stroke versus exhaustion, hypothermic arrest, delayed pulmonary decompensation after immersion, dysrhythmia and rhabdo after electrical injury.

# HOST-DEFINED — the patient's context is the diagnosis

- **Dialysis patient, any complaint** — Killers: hyperkalemia, missed sessions and volume overload, access infection or bleeding, pericardial effusion, uremia. Ask when they last dialyzed and whether the session ran to completion.
- **Cancer patient, any complaint** — Killers: neutropenic fever, cord compression, SVC syndrome, hypercalcemia, tumor lysis, PE, brain metastasis. Ask the date of the last chemotherapy.
- **Transplant or immunosuppressed** — Killers: opportunistic infection with a blunted presentation, rejection, drug toxicity and interactions. Fever with otherwise normal vitals still counts.
- **Sickle cell** — Killers: acute chest syndrome, splenic sequestration, aplastic crisis, stroke, priapism, osteomyelitis. Ask how this compares to their usual crisis and what dose usually works.
- **HIV / advanced immunosuppression** — Killers: CNS infection, PJP, TB, IRIS. Ask the last CD4 and whether they are on therapy.
- **IV drug use with fever** — Killers: endocarditis, epidural abscess, septic emboli, necrotizing infection, wound botulism.
- **Pregnant beyond 20 weeks, any complaint** — Killers: preeclampsia and eclampsia, abruption, PE, appendicitis in an atypical location, sepsis. Get gestational age and fetal movement first.
- **Postpartum, up to 6 weeks** — Killers: preeclampsia and eclampsia, postpartum hemorrhage, endometritis, PE, peripartum cardiomyopathy, sepsis.
- **Device problem — pacemaker or ICD, LVAD, VP shunt, trach, G-tube, Foley, ostomy** — Killers: ICD shocks and the arrhythmia behind them, shunt failure, LVAD thrombosis or pump stop, trach obstruction or decannulation, tube dislodgement through a fresh tract.
- **Nonverbal, demented, or group-home patient** — Killers: everything painful they cannot report. Occult fracture, retention, obstipation, pressure ulcer with sepsis, medication effect, neglect. Ask the caregiver what specifically changed and when.
- **Arrived from a facility, another ED, or custody** — Killers: whatever the transfer was actually for. The transfer reason is the most important sentence in the chart and is usually missing; the paperwork and the collateral leave with the crew, so get both before they go. The facility also constrains disposition — ask what they can and can't take back.
- **Recent procedure or surgery** — Killers: bleeding, infection, anastomotic leak, PE, retained material, and the complication specific to that procedure in its first 30 days.

# CROSS-CUTTING CLAUSES

These fire on many complaints. Listed once here rather than duplicated onto
twenty launcher lines — append the clause to whichever launcher you're sending.

- **New, stopped, or dose-changed drug in the last 8 weeks** → rash, fever with rash, weakness or AMS, seizure, syncope, nausea and vomiting, hypotension or bradycardia.
- **Anticoagulant name plus last-dose *time*** (the time is the part that matters) → head injury, elderly fall, headache, back pain with any neuro symptom, GI bleed, stroke, syncope.
- **Aortopathy, personal or family** — an ADD-RS predisposing condition → chest, back, abdominal, or flank pain.
- **Any reproductive-age patient with a non-obstetric complaint** → "Any chance you're pregnant, when was your last period, and have you delivered, miscarried, or had an abortion in the last six weeks?" hCG gates imaging and drugs; tubal ligation and an IUD raise the ectopic fraction rather than excluding pregnancy; preeclampsia and peripartum cardiomyopathy run six weeks out.
- **Rate-blunting — a STOP-IF clause, not a question**: beta blocker, rate-controlled AF, pacemaker, transplanted heart, cord injury. A normal heart rate is not reassurance in shock, sepsis, or bleeding.

# UNEXPECTED EVENTS — the 30-second version

Not previsits. Same engine, but you are walking into something already
happening, so the guide becomes what to ask the people in the room and what to
establish in the first minute.

- **Undifferentiated hypotension or shock** — Frame: which shock? Hypovolemic or hemorrhagic, cardiogenic, obstructive (PE, tamponade, tension PTX, auto-PEEP), distributive (sepsis, anaphylaxis, adrenal, neurogenic), toxic (CCB, beta blocker). Ask the team for the last known pressure, what is running, what has been given, and what access exists.
- **Acute hypoxia or respiratory failure** — Frame: airway obstruction, pneumothorax, flash pulmonary edema, PE, mucus plug or trach obstruction, opioid effect, equipment failure. Ask what changed in the last 10 minutes.
- **Cardiac arrest, inbound or in-department** — Frame the reversible causes as questions for EMS, family, and the nurse: down time, witnessed or not, bystander CPR, initial rhythm, insulin or dialysis, ingestion, trauma, pregnancy, temperature, anticoagulation.
- **Unresponsive, no history** — Frame: glucose, opioid, hypoxia, hypercarbia, ICH or stroke, postictal, sepsis, tox, hypothermia, hypotension. Ask EMS and family what the scene looked like and what medications are in the house.
- **Sudden deterioration of a boarded or roomed patient** — Frame: what has changed since someone last laid hands on them, what was given in the interval, and what the working diagnosis assumed that may be wrong. Anchoring is the main hazard here.
- **Peri-intubation crash** — Frame: hypoxia, hypotension, esophageal or mainstem placement, pneumothorax, auto-PEEP, equipment. Ask for the pre-intubation pressure and saturation and what induction agent and dose were given.
- **Post-procedure complication** — Frame: bleeding, perforation, pneumothorax after a line, local anesthetic systemic toxicity, sedation-related hypoventilation.
- **Transfusion reaction** — Frame: hemolytic, TRALI, TACO, anaphylaxis, febrile nonhemolytic, bacterial contamination. Ask what was hanging, how long it ran, and what the vitals were before it started.
- **Anaphylaxis, inbound or in-department** — Frame: airway, whether epinephrine has been given and when, biphasic risk, ACE inhibitor angioedema as the mimic.
- **Status epilepticus** — Frame: duration, what has been given and at what dose, glucose, sodium, eclampsia, INH and other tox, structural cause, nonconvulsive status once the movements stop.
- **Stroke or STEMI activation** — Frame: last known well as a clock time, anticoagulation, the mimics (hypoglycemia, seizure, migraine, conversion; pericarditis, dissection, early repolarization), and the contraindications asked as questions rather than read as a checklist.
- **Major trauma activation** — Frame: mechanism and energy transfer, anticoagulation, the places blood hides, distracting injury, and whether something medical caused the trauma.
- **Violent or agitated patient** — Frame: medical cause first, scene safety, what has already been tried, restraint and sedation history, and the collateral you lose access to once they are sedated.
- **Multiple patients at once, or a departmental surge** — Frame: not a previsit. Ask which of these can wait, what single finding in each would change that, and what you can delegate and to whom.

# NOTHING FITS

When the presentation maps to nothing above, or the triage note is noise, use
this instead of forcing a card:

```
Adult ED previsit. This doesn't fit a standard complaint. Here's what I have:

[paste]

First: 5 questions that would convert this into an actual chief complaint, and the baseline-delta questions — what changed, when, and compared to what (ADLs, ambulation, mentation, appetite, continence).

Then run the standard previsit format on the two most likely framings, saying explicitly that they are framings and not diagnoses.

Then one history question each for the occult emergency sweep: ACS, occult infection including line or hardware, stroke including posterior circulation, ICH, medication effect or a new or changed drug, metabolic (glucose, Na, Ca, thyroid, adrenal), GI bleed or symptomatic anemia, PE, retention or obstipation, dehydration and AKI, elder abuse or neglect, undertreated pain.

And: what to ask family, EMS, or the sending facility if the patient can't give a history. Questions only. No workup, no plan.
```
