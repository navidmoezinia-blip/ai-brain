# Verbal consult / phone-call overview (on request)

Use for `verbal consult`, `phone consult`, `call overview`, `chronological overview`, `walk me through the case for this call`, or a request for a longer, more detailed patient overview to use while speaking with another clinician or consultant. Do not append it routinely to notes, attending presentations, pager messages, or post-note updates. This one-off output leaves the encounter's BEFORE NOTE/POST-NOTE mode unchanged.

Produce one copyable `text` block with short, speakable bullets following the patient's chronology. Give enough connected detail to talk through the case; no 360-character limit or fixed word quota. Tailor content to the receiving service. Synthesize each meaningful turn; use only decision-relevant numbers in parentheses after the interpretation. More detail means useful chronology and reasoning, not a PMH inventory, a series of lab values, disconnected system lists, or a transcript of internal reasoning.

Use this sequence, omitting empty parts:

1. **OPENING:** age/sex, chief concern, and concise reason for the call; communicate supplied urgency early.
2. **RELEVANT BACKGROUND:** only the history, procedures, medications, or dated prior evaluations that change the current question. Prefer a brief clause integrated into the opening or chronology; do not force a separate PMH section.
3. **CHRONOLOGICAL OVERVIEW:** symptom onset and progression → relevant events/care before arrival → condition and findings on arrival → workup, treatments, and reassessments in their supported order. At each meaningful turn, connect what was found, why the evaluation/treatment mattered, what actually happened, and the supplied response or change in thinking. Include discriminating positives/negatives, relevant result trends, and important consult input.
4. **CURRENT STATUS / ASSESSMENT:** latest supplied condition, working explanation, unresolved concerns, and pending items that affect the consultant's decision.
5. **SPECIFIC ASK:** the evaluation, recommendation, procedure, admission decision, or other help being requested from this service; use the clinician's explicit ask when supplied.

- Synthesize from available notes, dotphrase data, and placed orders without requiring the clinician to narrate the sequence or explain every order. Infer clinically supported connections and order rationale; explicit clinician statements override inference.
- Chronology must come from supplied onset/dates/times and established sequence, not paste order. Use broad temporal anchors when exact times are absent; do not fabricate intervals, causal links, or a sequence between events whose order is unknown. Keep old encounters and baselines distinct from today's events.
- Preserve planned/ordered/performed/administered and preliminary/final status. Do not turn ordered treatment into given treatment, infer a response, or claim a consultant accepted the patient. Important uncertainty stays explicit and concise.
- Include the clinical sequence within this overview, but **do not create an ED COURSE field or an MDM ADDENDUM**. Do not add the pager callback line unless requested for the call script. New recommendations or consequential verification/citations stay outside the copyable block under the core rules.
- This prepares what the clinician can say; it does not place a call or contact a service.
