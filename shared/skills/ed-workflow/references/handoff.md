# ED handoff / signout (on request)

For receiving ongoing care (`picking up`, `taking over`, `received signout`), use [Incoming pickup](pickup.md). This reference is for handing care onward; a bare `signout` / `handoff` request retains this meaning unless the context specifies receiving care.

Use when handing ongoing patient care to another clinician/team. Produce one copyable `text` block, usually four to six concise bullets; combine sections for a straightforward patient and expand only for consequential complexity. Lead with what the receiving clinician needs to watch or complete, before the case summary or history. This preserves I-PASS content while using the clinician's requested ED order, not a claim that this exact template has been independently validated in adult ED practice.

## Content order

1. **WATCH / PENDING FIRST:** begin with the most consequential takeover item. An active deterioration concern comes first, with the stated if-then action; otherwise lead with the pending result, task, or reassessment and what to do with it. Include both watch items and pending work when relevant, before any clinical backstory. Put important contingencies beside their trigger rather than burying them later. Preserve stated plans; identify newly suggested medical actions as recommendations and verify per core rules. Do not invent a watch concern, threshold, or reassuring absence just to fill a heading.
2. **REMAINING TO DO:** list other outstanding results, reassessments, treatments, or consult tasks with next actions. Include ownership/timing when supplied without inventing a named owner, due time, or acceptance. Distinguish pending from not ordered, preliminary from final, and ordered from given. Combine with the first item when simple.
3. **BRIEF CONTEXT:** age/sex, working problem, supported acuity/current state, and the key findings, treatment, and response explaining the priorities above. Include only history that changes care; useful values go in parentheses. Do not lead with this summary or a separate PMH section. Do not manufacture stability from incomplete data.
4. **DISPO:** anticipated destination, what must happen first, and relevant practical barriers. State whether a consult, accepting service, or placement is actually confirmed. If undecided, name the supplied decision still outstanding.
5. **CONFIRM:** a brief prompt for the receiver to repeat back the key task/contingency and clarify responsibility. This is a handoff prompt, never documentation that read-back or transfer of care occurred. Omit it when the user specifically requests only a charted summary or a shorter subset.

## Placeholder layout

```text
HANDOFF
- Watch for: [important change/concern] → [stated if-then action].
- Pending / to do: [result/task] → [next action; owner/time if supplied].
- Context: [age/sex, working problem, current state, key findings/treatment response] ([essential values only]).
- Dispo: [destination] pending [remaining conditions/barriers].
- Confirm: [critical task/contingency for receiver read-back].
```

The example's watch-first order applies when that is the priority; use pending-first when there is no supplied active watch concern or a pending action is more urgent. Drop empty items; do not fabricate data to complete headings. A consequential gap stays explicit and brief. For multiple patients, a short patient label may identify each block, followed immediately by that patient's watch/pending priorities; keep facts and actions separate. Do not append an ED COURSE field, MDM addendum, pager callback number, or lengthy consult chronology. Preserve the encounter's BEFORE NOTE/POST-NOTE mode; drafting a handoff does not complete or communicate it.

## Basis and limits

- [AHRQ TeamSTEPPS I-PASS, reviewed July 2023](https://www.ahrq.gov/teamstepps-program/curriculum/communication/tools/ipass.html): acuity, concise summary, actionable tasks, contingency planning, and receiver synthesis.
- [AHRQ TeamSTEPPS Handoff, reviewed May 2023](https://www.ahrq.gov/teamstepps-program/curriculum/communication/tools/handoff.html): uncertainty, treatment response, responsibility, and acknowledgment during transfer.
- [ACEP Rapid Integration of Care Toolkit, January 2015](https://www.acep.org/siteassets/uploads/uploaded-files/acep/clinical-and-practice-management/resources/rapid-integration-of-care-toolkit_jan2015.pdf), Safer Sign Out section: ED pending items, anticipated disposition, and mutual understanding.

Use these as the communication framework; verify new clinical management advice separately. Do not paste framework citations into every clinical handoff unless requested or needed to explain a recommendation. Follow a supplied local handoff format when the clinician requests it.
