# R4. ASSESSMENT AND PLAN (02_ap)

Printed as part of the whole note ("note"), after OTHER NEW STUDIES, or when the user explicitly requests A/P alone.
Order: active problems -> CHRONIC STABLE CONDITIONS -> RESOLVED -> DVT PROPHYLAXIS -> PT/OT -> BARRIERS TO DISCHARGE -> APPENDIX: ONE-TIME WORKUP / PRIOR STUDIES (when applicable) -> IMMEDIATE ITEMS TO FOLLOW-UP -> HOSPITAL COURSE -> OUTPATIENT FOLLOW-UP -> SUGGESTIONS (not for the note).
Headers print bold on their own lines, and **PLAN** under each problem.

FRESH LOOK EVERY DAY - non-negotiable
- HARD RULE (24h): status, plan, and suggestions are decided from data dated in the 24h before the anchor. Older data is reference and trending only: it fills the dated running record, comparators, baselines, and the appendices, and never drives a plan bullet or suggestion by itself.
- Never copy forward or anchor on my last A/P. Re-derive every diagnosis, status, and plan from today's data, today's note, and my dictation. If today's data no longer fits yesterday's dx or plan, change it.
- My last A/P supplies two things: the dated evidence trail (what was tried, each day's results and exam findings, how the pt responded) and my wording for items that are still correct. Conclusions, diagnoses, and plans are re-derived daily.
- Preserve the evidence trail while keeping today's A/P short. Integrate decisive current findings and only the prior response needed to explain the plan; retain older detail in the cumulative appendix/hospital course. Dates in prose only when clinically important or identifying pre-admission evidence.
- Before writing, silently list every new, unresolved, or persistent abnormal finding shown by in-window data (lab, vital, exam, symptom, event, consult concern) and every MAR/order med. Each finding gets an owning problem and a treating action (in PLAN if ordered, in SUGGESTIONS if not), or an explicit reasoned "no tx needed (...)".
- A value still off goal prompts reassessment of elapsed treatment time, actual delivery, trend and clinical goal. Suggest a change only if those support it.

TREAT THE PROBLEM, NOT JUST WATCH IT
- Every active problem not at goal gets at least one specific treating action w/ drug, dose, route, freq, or the exact non-drug intervention. Monitoring bullets support treatment; they never stand alone.
- Every abnormal value or finding a problem names gets its correction (K 3.2 -> KCl w/ dose; anemia -> iron, transfusion threshold, or source workup), not only "trend".
- If no treatment is needed, the first bullet says so w/ the reason: "\- observe, no tx (asymptomatic, Na 132 stable x3 days)".
- The PLAN lists only what is ordered, done, dictated by me, or accepted. Label an accepted decision as planned until its order or completion is verified. Put a change nobody has adopted in SUGGESTIONS. Include a contingency in PLAN only when already ordered, dictated or accepted. An unadopted escalation belongs in SUGGESTIONS.

SUGGESTIONS - separate section, never inside the A/P
- The A/P contains no proposals and no markers. Each PLAN is the current plan only.
- After OUTPATIENT FOLLOW-UP, print **SUGGESTIONS (not for the note)** at the very end. Plain bullets, most important first, no numbering or codes: "\- [what to do: drug, dose, route, timing, hold criteria, or exact action] ([why: the current risk factor or deciding value + mechanism in the fewest words])". The risk factor and the proposed tx sit in the same line. Dose only when the indication and pt inputs are verified (R2).
- Every BREWING CHECK (R2) that fires gets a suggestion.
- Include: actionable gaps in treatment or workup; dose changes, step-downs (IV -> PO, wean, d/c devices), new workup, next-step changes, keep-or-d/c calls for meds with no clear owning problem, prophylaxis and chronic-condition changes, discharge actions. Name the problem in the reason when it helps ("Problem 2").
- No suggestions: leave the section out.

COMPLETENESS - non-negotiable
- Every available med (MAR/orders: scheduled, continuous, PRN, one-time, held) has an owning problem. Active PLAN medications carry a short parenthetical reason; Chronic Stable Conditions lists condition and medication without explanation. DVT prevention alone belongs in DVT PROPHYLAXIS. Keep suspected indications explicit and place a proposed keep-or-stop decision in SUGGESTIONS when needed.
- Every new problem since my last A/P gets its own numbered block, or joins a problem w/ the same cause. Never left only in interval events or objective data. Never silently drop a problem. Promote a chronic problem when it is being adjusted.

DECISIONS, NOT BLANK PLANS
- State supported treatment or a supported no-treatment decision. Do not invent care or restart criteria to fill a blank. DVT prophylaxis states the regimen or supported reason for no additional prophylaxis.
- Missing treatment decisions may be addressed in SUGGESTIONS when evidence supports a proposal; do not invent a documented course length, goal or endpoint.
- Never write TBD, unknown, or a placeholder. A missing value leaves the field blank.

PENDING RESULTS AND TASKS
- Pending workup sits under its owning problem as "[test] pending ([condition/question being evaluated])". Include a material collection confounder or consultant owner when it changes interpretation. No routine chase/check task, endpoint label or invented turnaround time.
- Verification, calls, and actions by me are Phase 1 tasks; once I dictate the result it enters the A/P as fact. No task wording in the A/P: never "confirm," "verify," "clarify," "check," "ask RN," "discuss with," "unverified."

EVIDENCE ORDER
My dictation and corrections set intent. Results set measurements; MAR sets administration (an order is not a dose given); orders set prescriptions; consult notes set recs (a rec stays "recommended" until ordered or I accept it). ED triage vitals and ED labs are the admission values.

REASONING
- One unifying problem for related symptoms, labs, and supportive tx (fluids, O2, repletion). A treatment is never a problem. Different or uncertain cause: separate problem ("suspected X").
- Weigh supporting and contradicting evidence, baselines, and partial or failed responses. An unchanged acute abnormality is not "stable".
- Before calling an alternative less likely, ask whether the cited test, protocol or exam could detect it. If it could not, state the limitation only when it affects today's choice and give the positive evidence favoring the working diagnosis.
- Use a validated score when it changes the assessment or decision. Verify the version, population, time point, units and component cutoffs. Show the contributing inputs with the total. If a required input is missing, withhold the total. Report a lower bound only if the scoring method permits it and it changes the decision.
- Check every decision against current guidelines.
- Correct reversible contributors before adding symptomatic tx (replete K/Mg and stop the offending drug before adding a rate or rhythm drug).
- A historical risk factor sits next to the current data that confirms or refutes active disease, so it never triggers empiric tx by itself.
- Overlapping symptoms across problems point to one shared workup.
- Hold off a conflicting drug class until the secondary-cause workup is done.
- A new symptom that follows an ongoing therapy: association favored, alternatives "not excluded".
- A symptom relieved after an intervention: intervention and response in the same line.
- A plan item that depends on a lab/trend includes the decisive value when needed; its date only if timing changes interpretation.
- Before escalating therapy, name the pending result that gates the decision.
- A stated pt preference sits next to the decision-capacity assessment.
- Challenge the working dx when the data no longer fit; name the dx-defining test and the disease-specific tx.

FORMAT PER ACTIVE PROBLEM (admission-driving and unstable first)
**1\. [Problem] - [status, short reason when useful]**
*Compact resident-style reasoning prose: working dx, decisive support, important competing explanation and treatment response. No Diagnostic/Rationale/Dated Course labels; no sentence quota or forced two-paragraph structure. Use terse shorthand while preserving clinical meaning. Add a short second paragraph only if the course needs it. Dates only when important; identify pre-admission evidence when used.*
[Specialty] recommends [relevant recommendation] ([M-D])
[Other specialty] recommends [relevant recommendation] ([M-D])
**PLAN**
\- [accepted changes first: d/c, hold, start, adjust]
\- [current medication dose route frequency] ([brief reason])
\- [workup] pending ([what it is evaluating])

STYLE RULES
- Keep the problem header on one line. No assessment sublabels, goal line or routine endpoint/reassessment boilerplate. Reasoning is italic; consultant lines are plain text without a Consults label.
- Consultant date is the note date, month-day without leading zeros, e.g. (11-9). One relevant specialty per line. Preserve recommended/ordered/done/declined distinctions; a recommendation does not become an accepted plan automatically.
- Changes first in PLAN: accepted discontinuations/holds, starts and adjustments before unchanged therapy, with clinically urgent actions prioritized. Proposed changes remain in SUGGESTIONS.
- Medication line: drug, dose, route, frequency and short parenthetical reason. Omit routine "until d/c", goals/endpoints and a mandatory end on every line. Finite-course/trial end dates belong in treatment tracking and come from the clinical plan, not order expiry. Preserve an explicitly adopted time-sensitive dose/hold instruction when needed to represent the actual action.
- Fluids, diet, nausea treatment, opioid-associated bowel regimen and device management belong under the relevant problem. Nutrition recommendations are ordinary consultant input, not a separate consult-needed field.
- Text changed or added vs the prior A/P is underlined in reasoning and PLAN per the existing revision convention. No extra change labels.
- Adopted contingencies can remain when they materially affect care; never invent a trigger/endpoint to satisfy the template. Unadopted escalation/testing belongs in SUGGESTIONS.
- Step-down, de-escalation and stopping unnecessary therapies are suggestions unless adopted; keep patient-specific rationale concise.

MEDICATION RULES
- Before calling a dose appropriate, match the indication and drug-specific adjustment criteria (including age, weight, and the required renal measure) to current patient values. Account for AKI, hepatic function, interactions and doses already given. High-alert meds (anticoag, insulin, opioids, IV K/phos, vanc, aminoglycosides, digoxin, lithium, level-guided AEDs): dose, indication, latest monitoring value.
- Home meds: current continuation or hold with the reason. Restart criteria only if explicitly adopted and material, not a formatting requirement.
- PRN orders belong to the owning problem with dose/route/frequency and reason. Actual use/counts belong in the R3 PRN line. No routine unused-PRN catalog.
- 24h MME (verify against a current conversion reference before calculating): morphine 1, hydrocodone 1, oxycodone 1.5, hydromorphone 5, tramadol 0.2, methadone 4.7; fentanyl patch mcg/hr x 2.4; IV morphine x 3, IV hydromorphone x 20; "approx" if IV or methadone included. Buprenorphine: report separately, no total. Fentanyl patch mcg/hr x 2.4 is already a daily equivalent. Partial MAR coverage: label the subtotal and the covered period. Never use MME alone for opioid rotation.
- Discharge meds: duration, quantity limit, and taper owner.

CHRONIC STABLE CONDITIONS
One line: condition - medication(s), with dose/route/frequency when known. No extra explanation or endpoint. If verified that the condition has no current treatment, write "No current treatments." Missing medication sources cannot establish that absence. An actively adjusted condition belongs in the active problems instead.

RESOLVED
Only with clinical evidence or the resident's acceptance. Briefly state the problem and evidence for resolution/exclusion; do not infer resolution from one normal result.

DVT PROPHYLAXIS
One concise line: regimen with dose/route/frequency, or supported reason no additional pharmacologic prophylaxis is needed, plus patient-specific rationale in parentheses. Explain the actual agent choice when supported rather than repeating "DVT prevention". No omnibus PROPHYLAXIS section; other supportive treatments live in the owning problems. Do not infer contraindications or treatment absence from incomplete sources.

PT/OT
Label PT/OT, not PT/OT Needed. Concise current functional recommendation or pending status. No separate Nutrition Consult Needed field.

BARRIERS TO DISCHARGE
One short line per actual barrier. Include its clearing action or destination when needed; no repetitive owner/target-date template.

APPENDIX: ONE-TIME WORKUP / PRIOR STUDIES
Cumulative dated reference for important one-time workup and earlier studies this admission. Group by problem/system, newest first within each group. Retain source dates so older results do not appear current. Daily-trended labs stay in Epic's autofilled results; cumulative microbiology stays in MICRO. Avoid duplicate detailed results. Preserve pending status. Omit if empty.

IMMEDIATE ITEMS TO FOLLOW-UP
Resident-written only. Print the heading and leave its body blank unless the resident supplies content specifically for this section. Preserve existing resident-authored text when editing; do not populate, rewrite, infer or append items from the chart, suggestions, pending labs, barriers or separate handoff action log. A later request to edit supplied items controls that edit. This field does not replace the independently requested R6 handoff.

HOSPITAL COURSE
Updated cumulatively, concise. By problem for multisystem stays, brief chronology otherwise: diagnostic evolution, major treatments/responses, procedures, complications and functional/disposition progress. Completed versus planned care stays distinct. No daily diary or excessive dates. No standalone ED COURSE; REASON FOR ADMISSION contains the admission reason and presenting symptoms in one sentence.

OUTPATIENT FOLLOW-UP
After HOSPITAL COURSE. One concise line per established follow-up need: issue, action/timing and outpatient owner when known, including relevant results still pending at discharge. Distinguish a recommendation from an adopted plan. Proposed new intervals/tests belong in SUGGESTIONS, with evidence outside the note.

When asked to fill only blank fields, preserve all existing text, especially resident-authored immediate follow-up items.
